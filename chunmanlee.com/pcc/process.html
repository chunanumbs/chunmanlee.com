Topic 01 / AI-enabled innovation

Build capability before chasing novelty.

As senior owner of an organization-wide AI initiative for product experience and design, I treated AI adoption as an operating-model challenge: define the outcome, evaluate fit, establish guardrails, equip teams, and measure where automation reduces production time and rework.

01
Problem / opportunity

Move from experimentation to responsible enterprise use

Teams needed practical ways to use AI across UX design and product management without fragmenting the brand, bypassing design-system standards, or weakening human judgment.

Role / leadership

Strategy, feasibility, governance, and adoption

I authored company-wide product design AI MBOs, led technology and design-feasibility assessments, founded a TED-style internal AI forum, and designed custom agents and low-code workflows.

Partnership

Shared ownership across Design, Product, and Engineering

Design defines experience quality and workflow fit; Product connects use cases to value; Engineering validates feasibility, integration, security, and scale. The operating model makes those decisions visible.

Insights / data

Evaluate tools against real work

V0, Figma Make, Aha! Elle, Anthropic, ChatGPT, and Copilot Pro were assessed against practical design and product workflows—not demo conditions.

Outcome / impact

Faster production with fewer avoidable loops

Markdown-based prototyping guardrails enforced brand DSL and component compliance. Applied agents and workflow automation measurably reduced design production time and rework.

Lesson

AI magnifies the operating system around it

Strong standards, clear accountability, reusable knowledge, and measurable outcomes turn AI into capability. Weak foundations simply produce inconsistency faster.

Frame

Business outcome

Choose the workflow and define value, risk, and ownership.

Evaluate

Feasibility + fit

Test tools against genuine team needs and system constraints.

Govern

Guardrails + DSL

Encode brand, accessibility, components, and review expectations.

Scale

Enable + measure

Share patterns, automate repeatable work, and track impact.

Problem / opportunity

Executive alignment through delivery

Leadership ideology & process

Design thinking is a mindset before it is a sequence of activities.

“These processes can be adapted at the macro or micro level, regardless of an engagement’s size or complexity. UX success depends on choosing the right mix of qualitative and quantitative research, usability, accessibility, human-computer interaction, technology context, business goals, project management, collaboration, and communication.”

Why the Double Diamond

I value the Double Diamond because it reflects how real work moves between exploration and focus. Diverging and converging makes the scale of information visible—how teams gather it, synthesize it, and apply it—and gives mature design organizations a practical model to work toward.

The flow of learning

The cyclical ebb and flow of data—and how each phase feeds the next—is crucial. Teams move from “Don’t Know / Could Be” and “Designing the right thing” toward “Do Know / Should Be” and “Designing things right.” Business owners need to see why that shift requires dedicated time and resources.

Scaling the organization

Formalize the system so people can do their best work.

As a people leader for two decades, I have built UX competencies from scratch and optimized existing UX and Product organizations. The right model varies by maturity and by whether design operates horizontally across departments or within mixed-competency teams.

“Formalizing documentation, balancing budgets, consolidating technologies and software, and creating employee-experience governance protocols are crucial to the success of any team.”
